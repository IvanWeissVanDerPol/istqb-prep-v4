## 💸 How to GET PAID in USD/EUR from Paraguay (the mechanics)

This is the part most guides skip. Let me be very specific.

### Step 1: Get a foreign bank account or payment platform

You need somewhere to receive USD/EUR. Options:

| Platform | Best for | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Payoneer** | Freelancers, Upwork, Toptal | Free to receive | Most common for LATAM. Has PY-friendly setup. |
| **Wise (TransferWise)** | Receiving from EU, converting currency | ~1-2% per transfer | Best for EUR. Has "borderless account" with USD + EUR + PYG. |
| **Deel** | Full-time remote contracts | Free for worker | Handles contracts, payments, and even taxes. |
| **Remote.com** | Like Deel | Free for worker | Some employers use this directly. |
| **PayPal** | Backup option | 5-6% fees | Most companies avoid it. Worst fees. |

**My recommendation:** Set up **Payoneer + Wise** before you start applying. Both are free to open.

### Step 2: Get a US/EU bank account (optional but powerful)

**Some platforms give you a US bank account:**
- **Payoneer:** Gives you a US bank routing number → you can receive ACH transfers from US companies
- **Wise:** Gives you US + UK + EU account details → receive like a local
- **Mercury** (if you form a US LLC): US bank account

With these, a US company can pay you **like you're a US employee** (just via direct deposit). No wire fees, no crazy exchange rates.

### Step 3: Convert USD/EUR to ₲ (your local currency)

You have two options:

**Option A: Spend in USD/EUR directly (recommended)**
- Pay your local bills with a **debit card linked to Wise or Payoneer**
- Wise debit card works in Paraguay, gives you a good exchange rate
- Save the rest in USD

**Option B: Convert to ₲**
- Wise: ~1% fee (best rate)
- Payoneer: ~2% fee
- BCP / Itaú: standard bank rate (worst — typically 3-5% spread)

**Pro tip:** Keep some savings in USD. The guaraní depreciates over time. USD savings = your future is protected.

### Step 4: Pay taxes (don't skip this!)

**This is important. Tax trouble is real trouble.**

Paraguay has favorable rules for foreign income:

- **IRP (Impuesto a la Renta Personal):** Foreign income is taxed if you bring it INTO Paraguay. If you keep it in Wise/Payoneer and spend via debit card, it's a gray area — **talk to a contador (accountant) about this.**
- **IVA:** You don't charge IVA on services you sell to clients abroad.

**Realistic tax cost for first job:**
- 8-10% of net income (with a good contador)
- Some people pay less by structuring smartly (legally)

**Find a contador** (accountant) in Asunción who knows about freelance/digital work. Ask in the ASOLINFO Paraguay group for recommendations. Budget USD 50-100/month for them.

### Step 5: Get your contract right

**Before signing any contract**, make sure you understand:

- **Are you an employee or contractor?** Big tax difference.
- **What's the payment schedule?** Monthly? Per project? Per hour?
- **What's the notice period?** (Usually 2 weeks for contractor, 30 days for employee)
- **Who pays for tools/software?** Most companies pay for the testing tools.

**Deel and Remote.com handle all of this for you.** If the company uses Deel, you don't need to think about contracts — Deel does it.

## 📈 Salary negotiation (don't accept the first offer)

Most junior testers don't negotiate because they don't know they can. **You can.**

### What to say when they offer you $2,000/month:

> *"Thank you for the offer. Based on my research of LATAM QA rates, and given my ISTQB certification and portfolio, I'd like to discuss $2,400/month. Is there flexibility?"*

Three outcomes:
1. **They say yes.** You just made an extra $400/month = $4,800/year = ₲ 35M
2. **They counter at $2,200.** You win anyway.
3. **They say no.** You say "let me think about it" — they often come back with more.

**Always negotiate. Even 10% adds up over a career.**
